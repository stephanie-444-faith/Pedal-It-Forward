I'm 12, and Pedal It Forward is my mitzvah project. A mitzvah project is a way to help your community while you get ready for your bar or bat mitzvah.

How it works

Every bike gets a second life, and every rider can help the next one.

  1. 🚲

    You bring a bike

    Outgrown, rusty, flat tires, broken chain? We'll take it.

  2. 🔧

    We fix it up

    New tubes, brakes, or a chain, plus a safety check.

  3. 🎁

    It finds a new rider

    We give it for free to someone who needs a way to get around.

  4. 🐼

    They pedal it forward

    Riders learn to keep their bike running and help the next person.

Give a bike

Got a bike sitting in the garage? It could be somebody's ride to school or work.

What we take

  • Kids' and adult bikes of any size
  • Bikes that are broken, rusty, or missing parts
  • Helmets in good shape, locks, lights, and pumps
  • Spare parts: tires, tubes, chains, cables, and tools

Get a bike

If you or someone in your family needs a bike, just ask. Our bikes are free for people who need one. No catch.

What happens next

  1. Fill out the form so we know who the bike is for.
  2. We'll get in touch when we have a bike that fits.
  3. You come get your bike, and we'll show you the ABC Quick Check.

We fix bikes as fast as people donate them, so it might take a little while. We won't forget about you!

The ABC Quick Check

Do this before every ride. It only takes a minute.

A
Air. Squeeze the tires. They should feel firm, not squishy.
B
Brakes. Squeeze each lever. The bike shouldn't roll when you push it.
C
Chain. It should be clean, oiled, and not falling off. Spin the pedals backward to check.
Quick
Quick releases. Wheel levers should be closed tight.
Check
Check it. Ride slowly for a few seconds and listen for anything weird.

Questions

Do the bikes really cost nothing?

Yes. Bikes we give away are free. The only bikes we sell are at our occasional bike sales, and that money buys parts for more free bikes.

Will you take a bike that's really broken?

Yes! Even if we can't fix it, we can usually save parts from it to fix another bike.

Who can come to a workshop?

Anyone who wants to learn. Kids under 12 should bring a grown-up. You don't need your own bike or tools.

Can I volunteer?

We'd love the help. Send us a message and tell us what you'd like to do.

Why a panda?

Because a panda riding a bike makes people smile. Helping people smile is kind of the whole point.
